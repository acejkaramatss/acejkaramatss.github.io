Remember, Unite, Renew: ACEJ Karama TSS Commemorates the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi & AERG Iterero Pilgrimage

Commemoration & Educational Tour Muhanga Campus & Nyanza-Kicukiro ACEJ Administration, Staff & AERG Iterero

Every year, the ACEJ Karama TSS community joins the nation in solemn remembrance of the more than one million victims of the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi. Through educational memorial journeys and on-campus commemoration vigils, our institution reinforces a resolute commitment to historical truth, human dignity, and building a united, resilient Rwanda.

Part I: Solemn Commemoration Assembly at ACEJ Karama TSS Campus

On our campus grounds in Mushubati, Muhanga District, students, teachers, staff, and local government leaders gathered under white memorial marquees for a dedicated day of reflection, testimony, and prayer[cite: 28]. The commemoration brought together the school body with administrative representatives from both the Sector and District levels[cite: 28].

The ceremony commenced with a minute of silence honoring the innocent lives cut short in 1994[cite: 28]. Keynote speakers, including local elders and institutional leadership, recounted Rwanda’s historical trajectory, highlighting how divisive politics fractured society and how the extraordinary courage of the Rwanda Patriotic Front (RPF-Inkotanyi) stopped the genocide and restored peace.

Campus Memorial Vigil: Unity • Remembrance • Resilience Students seated in formal white uniforms on campus alongside teaching staff, distinguished guests, and local administrative authorities during the Kwibuka reflection assembly in Muhanga[cite: 28].
“Educating technically capable youth is only half of our duty; our higher purpose is forming responsible citizens who value every human life, reject genocide ideology, and protect the hard-won peace of our nation.”

Throughout the gathering, student representatives offered poems, choral hymns of comfort, and reflective drama pieces[cite: 28]. The assembly concluded with a reaffirmation of the national pledge, “Never Again”, inspiring the student body to build careers rooted in civic integrity and mutual respect.

Part II: AERG Iterero Pilgrimage to the Nyanza-Kicukiro Genocide Memorial

As part of deeper historical education, student members of AERG Iterero (Association des Etudiants Rescapés du Genocide) at ACEJ Karama TSS undertook a guided educational pilgrimage to the historic Nyanza-Kicukiro Genocide Memorial in Kigali[cite: 27].

Nyanza-Kicukiro holds profound historical significance in Rwanda's collective memory. In April 1994, thousands of Tutsi sought refuge at the École Technique Officielle (ETO Kicukiro) under the protection of the United Nations Assistance Mission for Rwanda (UNAMIR). Following the abrupt withdrawal of UN peacekeepers on April 11, 1994, over 2,500 Tutsi men, women, and children were marched up the hill to Nyanza and systematically murdered by the Interahamwe militia and government soldiers.

Accompanied by school chaperones and AERG coordinators, ACEJ Karama TSS students toured the memorial grounds, examined historical documentation, and laid wreaths upon the mass graves where more than 105,000 victims now rest in honor.

AERG Iterero ACEJ Karama TSS Delegation at Nyanza-Kicukiro Student members of AERG Iterero wearing custom cream committee shirts and burgundy school uniforms, standing proudly beside the soaring metallic memorial flame sculptures alongside school staff and advisors in Kigali[cite: 27].

Preserving Memory • Empowering the Next Generation

For our students, standing in front of the sweeping commemorative sails at Nyanza was both an emotional and transformative experience[cite: 27]. The visit brought historical lessons to life, demonstrating the irreversible cost of ethnic hatred and the preciousness of national unity[cite: 27].

AERG Iterero within ACEJ Karama TSS continues to provide mutual psycho-social support, academic solidarity, and remembrance activities for student survivors, ensuring that the legacy of memory leads directly to purposeful learning, moral clarity, and national service[cite: 27].
